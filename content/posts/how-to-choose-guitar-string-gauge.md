String gauge is the thickness of each string, measured in thousandths of an inch. A set is usually named by its thinnest and thickest strings, so a "12–53" set has a 0.012" high E string and a 0.053" low E string. Gauge affects playability, volume, tone and even how well your guitar stays in tune.


## Common acoustic gauges

| Name | Typical range | Feels | Sounds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Extra light | 10–47 | easiest to press and bend | thinner, less volume |
| Custom light | 11–52 | easy, a good beginner choice | balanced |
| Light | 12–53 | the most common factory set | full and clear |
| Medium | 13–56 | firmer under the fingers | louder, more bass |

## Common electric gauges

| Name | Typical range | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Super light | 9–42 | easy bends, lead playing, beginners |
| Regular light | 10–46 | the all-round standard |
| Medium / heavy | 11–48 and up | fuller tone, lower tunings |

## How gauge changes your guitar

**Lighter strings** need less finger pressure, so they're easier for beginners and for wide bends. They produce a little less volume and bass, and they can buzz more if your action is very low or you strum hard.

**Heavier strings** put more tension on the neck. They sound louder and fuller, hold tuning better when you dig in, and suit drop tunings. They're harder on fingertips, especially early on.

## How to pick

1. **Start with what's on the guitar now.** If it plays well, stay within one step of that gauge.
2. **Beginners:** try extra light or custom light on acoustic, and 9s or 10s on electric.
3. **Strummers and bluegrass players:** light or medium for projection.
4. **Fingerstyle players:** custom light or light for a balance of comfort and clarity.
5. **Down-tuned rock and metal:** step up to 11s or a heavy-bottom set so the low strings don't flop.
6. **Vintage or delicate guitars:** stay light. Medium sets add a lot of tension to the neck and top.

## Do you need a setup after changing gauge?

A small change (for example 12s to 11s) usually needs nothing. A bigger jump can change neck relief, and on electrics it can affect intonation and the tremolo balance. If you notice new buzzing or the action rises, a quick truss rod adjustment or a professional setup will fix it.

## Quick answer

If you just want a safe choice: **light (12–53) for acoustic** and **regular light (10–46) for electric**. Adjust from there based on comfort.
