Changing your strings for the first time can feel intimidating, but it's a 20-minute job once you've done it once or twice. This guide covers a standard steel-string acoustic with bridge pins.


## What you need

- A new set of strings in your usual gauge (see [how to choose string gauge](/how-to-choose-guitar-string-gauge))
- A string winder, ideally one with a built-in pin puller
- Wire cutters
- A tuner (clip-on or a phone app)
- A soft cloth

## Step 1: Loosen and remove the old strings

Turn each tuning peg to slacken the strings until they're completely loose. Many beginners find it easiest to change one string at a time so the neck tension stays more even, but removing all six is fine too and lets you clean the fretboard.

## Step 2: Pull the bridge pins

Use the notch on your string winder to lever each bridge pin up gently. If a pin is stuck, push the string slightly into the guitar to free it. Keep the pins in order so you can return them to the same holes.

## Step 3: Clean the guitar

With the strings off, wipe the fretboard and the top around the bridge. This is the best chance you'll get to clean under the strings.

## Step 4: Fit the new string at the bridge

Put the ball end of the new string into its bridge hole and press the pin back in, with the pin's groove facing the string. Pull the string gently upward while pushing down on the pin so the ball end seats against the bridge plate inside the guitar. If the pin pops up when you tug the string, the ball end isn't seated yet.

## Step 5: Thread the string through the tuning post

Pull the string up to its tuning post. Leave about two to three inches of slack past the post (roughly the distance to the next post) so you get two or three wraps. Thread it through the hole and put a sharp kink in the string so it doesn't slip.

## Step 6: Wind it on

Turn the tuner so the string winds **downward** around the post, each wrap below the last. Keep light tension on the string with your other hand. On a standard acoustic, the bass strings wind counter-clockwise and the treble strings clockwise when you look at the front of the headstock, so the strings run along the inside of the posts.

## Step 7: Repeat for every string

Work through all six strings the same way.

## Step 8: Stretch and tune

New strings stretch, so they'll drift flat for a while. Tune each string to pitch, gently pull it away from the fretboard along its length, then retune. Repeat three or four times until the tuning holds.

## Step 9: Trim the ends

Cut the excess string about a quarter inch past the tuning post. Sharp ends can scratch you, so don't leave long pieces sticking out.

## Beginner tips

- Change strings every one to three months, or sooner if they look dull or feel gritty.
- Don't over-tension. If you're unsure which way to turn, tune down first and then up to the note.
- Dispose of old strings safely, since the ends are sharp.
