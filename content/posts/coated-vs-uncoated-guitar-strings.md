Walk into any guitar shop and you'll see two kinds of string packs side by side: regular (uncoated) strings and coated strings that cost noticeably more. Both work. The right choice depends on how often you play, how your hands react to strings, and how much you care about that bright "new string" sound.

## What a coating actually does

A coated string has a very thin polymer layer over the metal. On most brands the coating sits on the wound strings (the thicker bass strings), and sometimes the plain strings get a treatment too. The coating seals the gaps between the windings so sweat, skin oils and dirt can't settle in. That buildup is the main reason strings go dull and start to corrode.

Uncoated strings are bare metal: phosphor bronze or 80/20 bronze on acoustic guitars, nickel-plated or pure nickel on electrics.

## Tone

- **Uncoated:** brighter, with more shimmer and sustain in the first days. That brightness fades as the strings age.
- **Coated:** a little smoother and slightly less sparkly at first, but they keep that tone for much longer.

Many players can't tell the difference in a band mix. Recording players and fingerstyle acoustic players are the most likely to notice.

## Feel

Coated strings feel slicker under the fingers, and they usually make less finger squeak when you slide between chords. Some players love that. Others find them slippery, especially for string bends on electric guitar. Newer "ultra-thin" coatings feel very close to bare strings.

## Lifespan and cost

This is where coatings earn their price. A set of uncoated strings often sounds dead after a few weeks of regular playing. Coated sets commonly last several times longer. If you play daily or have sweaty or acidic hands, a coated set can cost less per month of playing even though the pack costs more.

## Who should buy which

**Choose coated strings if you:**
- play a guitar that sits out and gets picked up casually, so you don't change strings often
- have hands that kill strings quickly
- want consistent tone between gigs or recording sessions
- are a beginner who won't change strings every few weeks

**Choose uncoated strings if you:**
- change strings often anyway and love the fresh, bright sound
- want the most natural feel for bends and vibrato
- are on a tight budget per pack

## Making any string last longer

1. Wash and dry your hands before you play.
2. Wipe the strings with a dry cloth after every session.
3. Store the guitar in its case, away from humid rooms.
4. Don't over-tighten when tuning up. Tune up to pitch rather than down to it.

## The bottom line

If you're unsure, try one coated set and one uncoated set of the same gauge and note how long each sounds good to you. For most hobby players, coated strings are the better value. For players who restring every week or two, uncoated strings give the best tone for the money.
