A capo clamps across the fretboard so you can change key while keeping the same chord shapes. A cheap one can pull strings sharp or buzz. A good one disappears and lets you play.


## Capo styles

- **Trigger (spring) capos:** fastest to move between songs, one-handed.
- **Screw or adjustable-tension capos:** let you set exactly the pressure you need, which helps tuning stability.
- **Lever capos:** quick like a trigger, but with more even pressure.
- **Partial capos:** cover only some strings to create open-tuning sounds.

## Our picks

### Shubb C1
![Shubb capo](/uploads/2023/01/Shubb-Guitar-Capo.jpg)
A lever-action design that has been a favorite for decades. It clamps evenly and rarely knocks strings out of tune. **Best for:** steel-string players who want a reliable classic.

### G7th Performance 3 with ART
![G7th Performance 3 capo](/uploads/2023/01/G7th-Performance-3-Capo-with-ART.jpg)
G7th's Adaptive Radius Technology adjusts to the curve of your fretboard, so every string gets the same pressure. **Best for:** players who need perfect tuning, especially on stage.

### D'Addario NS Tri-Action
![D'Addario capo](/uploads/2023/01/DAddario-Guitar-Capo.jpg)
Spring action with a tension adjuster, a good mix of speed and control. **Best for:** an affordable all-rounder.

### Dunlop Trigger Capo
![Dunlop trigger capo](/uploads/2023/01/Dunlop-Acoustic-Trigger-Capo-for-Guitar.jpg)
A simple, sturdy trigger capo that is easy to move or park on the headstock. **Best for:** quick changes between songs.

### Ernie Ball Axis Dual Radius
![Ernie Ball Axis capo](/uploads/2023/01/Ernie-Ball-Axis-Dual-Radius-Capo.jpg)
A reversible pad fits flatter or rounder fretboards. **Best for:** players who swap between acoustic and electric.

### Paige Original
![Paige capo](/uploads/2023/01/Paige-Acoustic-Guitar-Capo.jpg)
Wraps around the neck and can stay on the guitar behind the nut when not in use. **Best for:** players who want a capo always on hand.

### Creative Tunings Spider Capo
![Creative Tunings Spider capo](/uploads/2023/01/Creative-Tunings-Super-Capo-for-Guitar.jpg)
Each string has its own lever, so you can capo any combination of strings. **Best for:** songwriters exploring partial-capo sounds.

## Tips for using a capo

1. Place it just behind the fret, not on top of it.
2. Use only as much pressure as you need. Too much pulls strings sharp.
3. Re-check tuning after putting it on.
4. Match the capo to your neck: classical guitars need a flat-radius capo.
