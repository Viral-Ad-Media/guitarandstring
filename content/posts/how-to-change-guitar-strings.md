Every guitar uses the same basic idea: anchor the string at the bridge, bring it up to the tuning post, wind it on and tune. The details change with the guitar. This guide covers all the common types.


## When to change strings

- The strings sound dull or lose sustain
- They look discolored or feel rough
- The guitar won't stay in tune or intonation drifts
- A string breaks (if one goes, the rest are usually old too)

Regular players typically restring every 4–8 weeks. [Coated strings](/coated-vs-uncoated-guitar-strings) last longer.

## Tools

String winder, wire cutters, a tuner and a cloth. For a Floyd Rose locking bridge you also need the right hex keys.

## Acoustic guitar (bridge pins)

1. Slacken and remove the old strings, then lift the bridge pins with a pin puller.
2. Put the ball end in the bridge hole, push the pin in and pull the string up until the ball seats against the bridge plate.
3. Pull the string to its tuner, leave two to three inches of slack, thread it through and kink it.
4. Wind downward so the wraps stack neatly, then stretch and retune.

Our [beginner's acoustic restringing guide](/how-to-change-guitar-strings-beginners) covers each of these steps in more detail.

## Electric guitar (standard bridge)

1. Remove the old strings. On guitars with through-body stringing, push the old strings out from the back.
2. Feed the new string through the bridge or body until the ball end seats.
3. At the headstock, leave enough slack for two to three wraps on the wound strings and three to five on the plain strings.
4. Wind downward, keep tension on the string, then stretch and tune.

**Locking tuners:** pull the string tight through the post, lock it, and tune up. You only need a partial wrap.

**Floating tremolo (Fender-style):** change one string at a time so the bridge stays balanced.

## Floyd Rose and other locking tremolos

1. Unlock the nut clamps first.
2. Change one string at a time. Cut the ball end off the new string, clamp it in the saddle block and tighten gently.
3. Wind it on at the headstock and tune roughly.
4. Rebalance the bridge with the spring claw screws in the back cavity if needed, tune with the fine tuners centered, then lock the nut and fine-tune.

Placing a thin card under the back of the bridge while you work keeps it from tilting.

## Classical guitar (nylon strings)

1. Tie the string at the bridge: pass it through the hole, loop it back around itself and tuck it under two or three times so it holds itself.
2. At the slotted headstock, pass the string through the roller hole, loop it under itself once and wind so it locks.
3. Nylon stretches a lot. Expect to retune often for a day or two.

## Common mistakes

- **Too few wraps:** the string can slip. Too many and the tuning becomes unstable.
- **Wrong winding direction:** strings should run along the inside of the posts.
- **Not stretching new strings:** the guitar will keep going flat.
- **Changing the gauge drastically without a setup:** expect buzz or high action. See [how to choose string gauge](/how-to-choose-guitar-string-gauge).
