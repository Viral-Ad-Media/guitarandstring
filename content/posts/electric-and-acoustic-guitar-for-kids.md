A child's first guitar should be the right size, easy to press down and good enough to stay in tune. A guitar that hurts to play is the fastest way to lose interest.


## Get the size right

| Child's age (approx.) | Guitar size |
|---|---|
| 4–6 | 1/4 size or mini (around 30") |
| 6–9 | 1/2 size (around 34") |
| 9–12 | 3/4 size (around 36") |
| 12+ | full size, or a smaller-bodied full-scale guitar |

Height and arm length matter more than age. If you can, let your child hold the guitar before buying.

## Acoustic, classical or electric?

- **Classical (nylon strings):** the softest on small fingers. Great for very young beginners.
- **Steel-string acoustic:** louder and brighter, but needs more finger pressure. Use light strings.
- **Electric:** surprisingly easy to play thanks to thin strings and low action. You'll need a small amp and headphones.

Let the child's musical taste guide you. A kid who loves rock will practice more on an electric.

## Tips for parents

1. **Budget for a setup.** Cheap guitars play much better after a music shop lowers the action.
2. **Use light strings** on steel-string guitars.
3. **Get a clip-on tuner.** An out-of-tune guitar sounds bad no matter how well you play.
4. **Keep it out of the case and on a stand** so it's easy to pick up.
